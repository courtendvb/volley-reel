Guide

How a video is built. Three layers, one MP4.

A video is a list of scenes. A scene is a set of clips sorted under up to five markers. Learn these three and you know how every video in the app is put together.

The three layers

Video, scene, marker.

Each layer answers one question. The video: what do we show today? The scene: which topic? The marker: which kind of play inside that topic? The clips are the plays themselves.

  • Video

    What you export and play in the meeting: scenes in the order you set. Each video has its own look, with colours, scoreboard, captions and title cards. A project can hold as many videos as you like, such as one for the players and a shorter one for the staff.

  • Scene

    One topic, like a rotation, a player or an opponent's setter. A scene can sit in more than one video. Fix a clip once and every video that uses the scene shows the change.

  • Marker

    Up to five coloured groups inside a scene, each with a name and its own title card. Press 1 to 5 on a play to put it under that marker. One clip can carry two markers, and a marker can be switched off without losing its clips.

  • Clip

    One play from the scout file, cut around the moment. Trim it, slow it down, hold a frame, and draw on it. Slides sit between clips as still pages: a frame, a PDF page or a data slide.

The path

From the scout file to the MP4.

Matches go into the library once. Everything after that reuses them, so next week's video starts from the same files.

  1. LibraryImport each match once: the video with its .dvw or .vsm.
  2. ProjectPick your team and the matches to use. Add more as the season goes on.
  3. SearchFilter every play across those matches by player, skill, rotation, zone or call.
  4. ScenePress 1 to 5 on a play to add it under that marker, or add all results at once.
  5. VideoPut scenes in order, name the markers, turn title cards on, add slides.
  6. ExportThe whole video, each scene, one scene or one marker, as MP4 with chapters.

Short on time? Make from a preset builds the whole video in one step, with a scene per rotation, player or middle and markers by reception or result. You can still change anything afterwards.

An example

A side-out video, layer by layer.

The coach wants to show how the team sides out in each rotation, and what changes after a poor pass.

  1. One video"CRO side-out", with a title card that opens it.
  2. Six scenesOne per rotation, so the players see one rotation at a time.
  3. Two markers in eachMarker 1, after a good reception. Marker 2, after a poor one. Each opens with its own title card.
  4. The clipsThe attacks themselves, with the scoreboard, the attacker's caption and where they landed drawn on.
Alongside your scouting software

DataVolley and VolleyStation scout. This app tells.

Keep scouting the way you do. The app reads the .dvw or .vsm file they write and starts where their job ends: turning the scouted plays into video made for players.

A play list

Plays in match order, one after another. Right for the analyst at the desk, looking for something.

A video

Plays grouped by topic, titled, with the point drawn on them. Right for players in the room, who need to see it once and get it.

Built on what each does well

  • From DataVolleyDeep search, and several searches in one video

    Filter by player, skill, rotation, zone, tempo, combination and more, and save the filters for one click. A scene's five markers hold different kinds of plays in one video, across matches.

    A 1 to 5 button on every row of the results puts the play straight in. Clips and markers move by dragging.

Where making the video tends to take time, and what changes here

Both are excellent at scouting and we keep using them. These are the spots where, over several seasons with both, the video work took us the most time. Versions differ, so yours may already handle some of them.

    Widen the matches so a player with few plays has enough, and a busy player can end up with around a hundred clips to go through one by one.

    Here: add what you want

    Press 1 to 5 on the plays as you look down the list. When taking everything and trimming is quicker, add all results at once.

  2. DataVolley montageComing back to a finished video

    The choices made along the way aren't always there when you want to change one clip a week later.

    Here: every step is kept

    Which plays, their IN and OUT, the order and the drawings all save as you go. Open the project, change the clip, export again. Undo works, and deleted scenes are kept.

  3. VolleyStation exportOne length for every clip

    The seconds before and after the play are set once, for all clips together.

    Here: each clip its own

    Trim IN and OUT clip by clip, and add slow motion or a hold where it matters.

  4. VolleyStation exportNotes made in another program

    Text or an arrow means exporting, opening a video editor and exporting again.

    Here: draw on the clip

    Text, arrows, circles and shapes on the court go on in the app and into the same export.

  5. VolleyStation exportDifferent topics in one video

    Montages come out per search or preset, and putting them together takes extra steps.

    Here: scenes and markers

    Side-out, serve and one player's attacks sit in one MP4, each opening with its own title card.
